# Stage 6: Referee Simulation

**Agents:** `referee` + `referee-freeform` + `referee-mechanism` (launched in parallel — none sees the others' output)

Three referees with distinct mandates read the same paper cold. The structured `referee` gives a top-journal referee report with numbered comments and action tags. The `referee-freeform` gives an editorial publishability assessment — forest, not trees. The `referee-mechanism` evaluates whether the paper's {{MECHANISM_QUALIFIER}} mechanism actually delivers the claimed result through the claimed channel (complementary to math-auditor, which checks proof correctness at Gate 2 — mechanism referee checks whether the {{MECHANISM_DISCIPLINE}} the paper invokes is the {{MECHANISM_DISCIPLINE}} the math supports).

1. Delete any previous referee reports in `paper/simulated_referee_reports/` matching the pattern `*_v*.md` (i.e., `YYYY-MM-DD_vN.md`, `..._vN_freeform.md`, and `..._vN_mechanism.md`). Do **not** delete prior `editor_decision_r*.md` files — the editor reads them as historical context.
2. Launch all three referees in parallel (fresh context, no knowledge of development process). **Manuscript-only launch context:** do NOT include any seed, mechanism-contract, pivot-log, `process_log/`, or stage-output — neither the path nor a summary of its contents — in the referee launch prompts — referees evaluate only the submitted manuscript under `paper/` (see their bodies' "Read scope — manuscript only" sections; a comment a real referee could not have made from `paper/` alone is dropped by the editor under Rule 7). Provide save paths:
   - structured → `paper/simulated_referee_reports/YYYY-MM-DD_vN.md`
   - freeform → `paper/simulated_referee_reports/YYYY-MM-DD_vN_freeform.md`
   - mechanism → `paper/simulated_referee_reports/YYYY-MM-DD_vN_mechanism.md`
3. Commit: `pipeline: stage 6 — referee reports received (structured + freeform + mechanism)`

## Editor aggregation (between referees and Gate 5)

**Agent:** `editor`

The orchestrator does NOT read the three referee reports directly to form a Gate 5 verdict. The `editor` agent is the verdict aggregator — it reads the three reports + paper draft + scorer history + target tier + pipeline state and produces the canonical Gate 5 routing decision. This removes silent-aggregation failures (e.g., a Reject vote from the freeform referee being implicitly outvoted by Major Revision verdicts from the other two).

**Bypass recording (default mode too).** Launching fewer than the three referees, skipping the `editor` aggregation and forming a Gate 5 verdict yourself, or running any of these agents' tasks via a substitute is a core bypass *unless this doc sanctions it* — record a `gate-skipped` / `agent-substituted` row in `process_log/degradation_ledger.md` before continuing (`docs/core_bypass.md`).

4. Launch `editor` with:
   - The three referee reports from step 2
   - The current paper draft (`paper/main.tex` + `paper/sections/*.tex`)
   - Pipeline state: `process_log/pipeline_state.json` (editor reads `target_journal_tier`, `initial_journal_tier`, `loops.referee.round`, `scores`, `regeneration_round`, `seeded`)
   - Glob hint for prior editor decisions: `paper/simulated_referee_reports/editor_decision_r*.md`
   - Output path: `paper/simulated_referee_reports/editor_decision_rN.md` (N = current `loops.referee.round`)
5. Commit: `artifact: editor decision r{N}`

The editor produces a single file with three load-bearing fields:
   - **Aggregated verdict:** Accept / Minor Revision / Major Revision / Reject — this is what the Gate 5 table below routes on.
   - **Mechanism verdict pass-through:** the mechanism referee's verdict copied verbatim — the editor cannot override it.
   - **Canonical comment list:** the merged, deduplicated, conflict-resolved comment list across all three referees. **Triager runs on this list, not on the raw three reports.**
   - **Journal-fit verdict:** Keep target tier / Downgrade to {tier} / Upgrade to {tier}.

The editor's aggregation rules are spelled out in its agent body (`editor.md`). Summary for orchestrator routing:
- **Mechanism MISATTRIBUTED or DECORATIVE** → editor must set aggregated verdict to Major Revision; mechanism overrides apply downstream as before.
- **Any single Reject vote (structured or freeform)** → editor must set aggregated verdict to Reject. The one allowed escape: editor may downgrade to Major Revision + recommend Downgrade tier IF the rejecting referee's stated reason is journal-fit (not paper quality), with quoted tier-fit language in the justification.
- **Otherwise** → strict aggregation across structured + freeform (the stricter of the two wins).

## Journal-fit handling

If the editor recommends **Downgrade** (one rung down the variant ladder, `{{TIER_LADDER_PROSE}}`):

**A Downgrade is a target-tier rejection — it routes to deepen-toward-target, not to an immediate re-target.** The contribution failing to clear the current target is the same event as a Reject *of that target*, and it is handled the same way the Reject row is: the pipeline tries to lift the paper *to* the target before conceding the lower tier. Sorting down is the last resort, not the response. **Do NOT lower `target_journal_tier` or recompute the threshold at this point** — that happens only after a target-tier ceiling is certified (step 2b below). This applies to every Downgrade recommendation, whether it arose from the Rule 2 tier-fit escape from Reject or from Rule 3 aggregation paired with the Rule 5 quote gate.

1. **Build the deepen-toward-target directive.** The directive source is the editor's quoted tier-justification spans in `editor_decision_rN.md` — the two verbatim structural-ceiling spans under the Rule 5 quote gate, or the single rejecting referee's two-part tier-fit quote under the Rule 2 escape — **not** the referees' `## What would be publishable` sections (those are Reject-only and constrained to "keeping the core"). Unlike a Reject deepen, this directive **may require a core change** — enriching the model's binding primitive (e.g. a deterministic hazard → a stochastic one), adding a lever, endogenizing a fixed object — because the ceiling is a property of the current core, not a fixable gap within it.
2. **Launch `branch-manager` with context = `gate-5-downgrade`** (output path `paper/simulated_referee_reports/branch_manager_downgrade_rN.md`, N = `loops.referee.round`; inputs = `editor_decision_rN.md` with its ceiling spans + the theory draft history `output/stage2/theory_draft_v*.md` + `stage1_candidates` + `process_log/pipeline_state.json`). Branch-manager classifies the ceiling per its strengthened taxonomy (`branch-manager.md` §B) and does exactly one of:
   - **(2a) Names a core change that could plausibly lift the contribution to the current target** → it emits an *enrich-the-core* deepen directive. Pass it to `theory-generator` (mandate: enrich the core, keep the idea; re-fire `empiricist` / `experiment-designer` too if their extensions are active). Then **increment `loops.downgrade_enrich.round` in `pipeline_state.json` (treat a missing field as 0), increment `loops.referee.round` once, and re-run Stage 6 at the unchanged target tier.** This is the round's only `loops.referee.round` increment — the re-run starts fresh and its verdict-row does **not** re-increment for this same transition. The tier does **not** move on this branch. Termination: the next `gate-5-downgrade` launch reads `loops.downgrade_enrich.round` and judges, from the theory-draft history, whether the prior enrichment was *substantive* (a real core rebuild) or *cosmetic* (relabeled/restated). **Once `loops.downgrade_enrich.round >= loops.downgrade_enrich.cap`, branch-manager may no longer emit a fresh enrich directive — it must certify (2b)** on the named-candidate-exhaustion bar, using the two prior enrich attempts as the evidence that the core-change candidates are exhausted. The `loops.referee.round >= loops.referee.cap` cap is the outer backstop regardless. `loops.downgrade_enrich.round` resets to 0 whenever the tier actually moves (2b) or the paper exits the Stage 6 loop.
   - **(2b) Certifies a target-tier ceiling** — branch-manager has *named* the core-change candidate(s), each has been **attempted-and-failed or shown dead** (intractable, yields nothing new, or itself not novel), and **no further candidate can be named**. On a **seeded** run the search space is enrichments *within the seed's idea* (Restart/Regenerate is forbidden — the seed is the contract), so certification means "even the richest faithful development of the seed cannot reach the target tier." On an **unseeded** run, certification additionally requires that a Restart/Regenerate to a different idea is not the better path. → **Only now apply the Downgrade:** reset `loops.downgrade_enrich.round` to 0 in `pipeline_state.json`, update `target_journal_tier` one rung down, recompute the Gate 4 advance threshold (per `docs/stage_4.md`), then proceed as the contribution warrants — if the aggregated verdict is **Accept/Minor Revision** and the current score clears the new threshold, proceed to Stage 7 (ship at the lower tier); if **Major Revision**, continue the loop at the lower tier. This ship-at-lower-tier is the documented last resort, reached only on branch-manager's certification.
3. **Re-launching Stage 6 after an actual tier change** (only on the 2b branch, once the tier has moved). The referees' variant context is baked at deploy time and does not auto-update from `target_journal_tier`. To propagate the new tier, the orchestrator MUST include the following block in each referee's launch prompt on the next round: `> Tier override: the pipeline's active target tier has been updated to {new_tier} (variant tier examples — {{TIER_DOWNGRADE_EXAMPLES}}). Evaluate this paper's fit against that tier, not the original {old_tier} target. The bar for Accept/Minor/Major/Reject must be calibrated to the new tier. If, in your judgment, the contribution could clear a tier ABOVE {new_tier} — i.e., a prior downgrade was over-aggressive and the paper now merits a higher journal — say so explicitly and name the tier. That higher-tier signal is how the editor decides whether to upgrade the tier back toward the project's original target; without it the paper stays parked here.` Replace `{new_tier}` and `{old_tier}` with the actual values. Do this for `referee`, `referee-freeform`, and `referee-mechanism` on every round after the tier change, until the pipeline ships.
- Commit on the 2a branch: `pipeline: gate 5 — downgrade routed to deepen-toward-target (r{N})`. Commit on the 2b branch: `pipeline: gate 5 — target tier downgraded to {tier} per certified target-tier ceiling`.

If the editor recommends **Upgrade** (one rung up the variant ladder, `{{TIER_LADDER_PROSE}}`): same procedure as Downgrade with the higher tier — update `target_journal_tier`, recompute the Gate 4 threshold, and include the tier-override prompt block on subsequent referee launches. Upgrade is the mechanism that undoes an earlier over-eager downgrade: a paper parked below the project's initial (highest) target can climb back toward it when the revision path now supports it, so **restoring a paper toward its original target is a normal routing outcome, not a rare one**. Upgrading *above* the project's initial target is the genuinely rare case and requires clearing the editor's Rule 5 quote gate (two verbatim same-direction structural-ceiling spans from two different referees) in the above-initial-target direction. The early-exit symmetry: if the paper's most recent Gate 4 score **already clears the higher tier's threshold** and the aggregated verdict is Accept or Minor Revision, the orchestrator may proceed to Stage 7 at the higher tier (the paper has earned it). This is usually rare on Upgrade — a paper downgraded to `field` because it could not clear `{{INITIAL_TIER}}` will not already clear `{{INITIAL_TIER}}` without a revision cycle — so an Upgrade most often continues the loop at the higher tier. The one combination to route deliberately is **Accept (at the current lower tier) + Upgrade**: do not ship at the lower tier; raise the target and continue (or ship at the higher tier if the score already clears it). Shipping an Accept at a tier two referees said the paper exceeds is the under-selling the rescue-first design exists to prevent. **Anti-oscillation guard:** if a run has already reversed tier direction once (a Downgrade followed by an Upgrade, or vice versa), the editor must require clearly stronger cross-referee evidence for any further direction change and should otherwise Keep — repeated opposite-direction tier changes in one run signal a genuinely borderline contribution, not a tier that needs more adjusting. The 10-round Stage 6 cap bounds the loop regardless.

If the editor recommends **Keep**: no tier change; proceed with the aggregated verdict.

## Gate 5: Referee Decision

Read the editor decision file (`paper/simulated_referee_reports/editor_decision_rN.md`). The aggregated verdict + mechanism verdict drive routing per the table below. The triager (when fired) reads the editor's canonical comment list, NOT the raw three referee reports.

**Hard rules driven by the mechanism verdict.** The mechanism verdict interacts with core.md's never-abandon rule (once a paper exists at Stage 5+, the pipeline must produce a finished paper via the deepening playbook, not loop back to Stage 0). The rules below are compatible with never-abandon: neither MECHANISM-MISATTRIBUTED nor MECHANISM-DECORATIVE forces Stage 0. Instead, both route the paper through a mandated restructure-or-narrow path that respects the never-abandon envelope.

- **MECHANISM-VALID** — normal Gate 5 flow.
- **MECHANISM-PARTIAL** — normal Gate 5 flow; the mechanism referee's `[FIX]` items join the other two reports' `[FIX]` items in triage.
- **MECHANISM-MISATTRIBUTED** — forces **Major Revision** as the Gate 5 recommendation, overriding Minor/Accept from the other two referees. The paper must be rewritten around the actual driver the mechanism referee identified. The mechanism report's `[FIX]` items are **locked** — the triager cannot downgrade them under this verdict (see triager rule 3). Revision happens within the never-abandon envelope: the existing paper draft is restructured, not discarded. Deepening playbook applies if the new driver needs added mathematical content.
- **MECHANISM-DECORATIVE** — forces **Major Revision with restructure-or-narrow mandate**, overriding Minor/Accept. DECORATIVE does NOT force Reject, and does NOT route to Stage 0 — both would violate never-abandon. Instead, the theory-generator is given a mandate, **restructure-first**: (a) **restructure** — use the deepening playbook to find real {{MECHANISM_QUALIFIER}} content the simple model was hiding, and rebuild the paper's mechanism claim around it — is the **default**, attempted before any narrowing; (b) **narrow** — strip the mechanism framing and present the paper as a structural characterization of the identity the math actually delivers (scientist-first: a correct narrow claim beats a broad decorative one) — is the **fallback**, taken only when restructure has been attempted and surfaced no real {{MECHANISM_QUALIFIER}} content, or when the math demonstrably cannot support {{MECHANISM_QUALIFIER_AN}} mechanism (not merely that one has not been found yet). Do not reach for narrow at round 1 because it is cheaper. If after the 10-round cap the verdict remains DECORATIVE, the paper ships with the narrow framing per (b) and a limitations paragraph documenting the original mechanism claim's failure — narrow is the principled terminal default, not the round-1 default. All mechanism `[FIX]` items are locked (see triager rule 3).
